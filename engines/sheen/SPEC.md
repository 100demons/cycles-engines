## 1. What SHEEN is

A **hybrid-rate metal percussion synth** built hat-first. Its primary job: at the home position (all four knobs at 64)
it plays an extremely modern, impressive, record-ready **closed hi-hat** with PUNCH off and **open hi-hat** with PUNCH
on, so one track plays a matched pair and a closed step **chokes** the open one, like a real hat pedal. Its secondary
job: every control is a **macro** that drives 6–11 internal parameters at once, and each knob leaves the home hats in
two directions, so the same engine also plays 808 and 909-style hats, pedal chicks, trap roll hats, shakers,
tambourines, rides, crashes, cowbells, reversed cymbals, gongs, laser chirps and crushed lo-fi hats.

Hard constraints:

- **CPU**: the worst-case block (§8) costs **no more than the stock TONE machine** measured in the same emulator run.
  Target **≤ 0.90 × TONE**, to leave margin: the emulator counts instructions, not cycles.
- **Rates**: the expensive shaping runs at **24 kHz** (16 samples per 32-frame block). The metal oscillators, the
  noise, the tick and the air run at the full **48 kHz** (§3). Unlike CRACK, a hi-hat cannot live in a 24 kHz core:
  its identity is between 6 and 18 kHz, and 24 kHz stops at 12 kHz. Don't move the 48 kHz layer down without the owner
  (§11), except as the last step of the cut ladder (§8).
- **Arithmetic**: integer C99 only. No float, no 64-bit arithmetic, no libc, no division in per-sample code (one
  division per block is fine), no EMAC inline assembly. This keeps the host build and the device build bit-identical.
  No signed overflow anywhere: phases are `uint32_t`, everything else is clamped.
- **Size**: code + tables ≤ **6 KB** target (report the real number); tables ≤ **2 KB**; variables ≤ **640 bytes per
  voice**, shimmer buffer included (store cached macro outputs as 16-bit). The chip has only 8 KB of instruction cache
  and 8 KB of data cache, shared with the OS and the other engines (Modded-Cycles `notes/26` and `notes/27`), so size
  is CPU.
- **Name**: `SHEEN` (engine names are 5 letters or fewer). Package id `sheen`.

## 2. Platform facts

- CPU: NXP MCF54415, ColdFire V4, ~250 MHz, no FPU. A load that misses the data cache costs tens of cycles
  (Modded-Cycles `notes/23`), so keep per-sample memory traffic to the voice's own state and its shimmer buffer.
- The voice-loop contract is documented in the header comment of `engines/refmachine/refmachine.c`:
  `sheen_update(pmod, v, p)` then `sheen_render(out, v)`, once per block of 32 frames at 48 kHz, per track. `pmod` is
  the trig's note in semitones `<< 16`; parameters are 8.8 words at `p + 0x14` (PITCH), `0x16` COLOR, `0x18` SHAPE,
  `0x1a` SWEEP, `0x1c` CONTOUR, `0x1e` PUNCH, `0x20` GATE, `0x22` FINE, `0x24` DECAY.
- Copy the bridge pattern of `engines/punch/punch.c` (the owner's percussion engine, already working on hardware): it
  reads velocity from `0x40fe4cdc` (a 16-bit word per track, velocity `<< 8`; 0 means "not set" → use 100), PUNCH and
  GATE as on/off, detects the trig and the note end, detects the first block after a machine change, and skips silent
  voices. **Use its trig and note-end detection exactly as it does.** Take the pitch maths from `refmachine.c`
  (`phase_inc`), at 48 kHz for the oscillators.
- SHEEN makes its own envelopes and does **not** call the stock amp chain (AMP_ENV / VCA / PUNCH), as PUNCH does. It
  reads DECAY itself and uses it as a macro (§5.4).
- Output: `out[32]`, `int32_t`, Q31.
- CPU is measured with `tests/emu/mcengine.py` → `count_instructions()`: instructions per block, per voice. The
  default emulator trig is note 60 (`tests/emu/harness.py` → `play`). Stock machines are addressed through `MACH` in
  `tests/emu/mcengine.py` (`METAL = 2`, `TONE = 4`).
- The OS's voice loop halves each track in place after render (Modded-Cycles `notes/36`). When you compare the
  emulator with the host, reproduce that halving exactly (arithmetic shift right by 1); never widen the tolerance.

## 3. Signal flow

```
 trig, velocity, note, 8 knobs, PUNCH (latched at the trig), GATE
        │
        ▼
 MACRO MAPPER (per block; recomputed only when an input changes)  ──►  ~50 internal parameters
        │
 ┌──────┴──────────────────────────── 48 kHz: 32 samples per block ───────────────────────────────┐
 │ METAL  6 square oscillators (phase accumulators), SUM (808) ⇄ RING (XOR pairs) blend            │
 │ NOISE  32-bit LCG                                                                                │
 │ SOURCE = metal · m + noise · (1 − m)                                                             │
 │ TICK   SOURCE ─► 1-pole HP ─► × tick env (per sample)                                ─┐          │
 │ AIR    tick's HP output ─► 2nd 1-pole HP ─► × air env                                ─┤          │
 └──────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────┘
        │ every other SOURCE and NOISE sample (no decimation filter, on purpose: §4.3)   │
 ┌──────┴──────────────────────── 24 kHz core: 16 samples per block ────────────┐       │
 │ VOICE    SOURCE ─► 1-pole pre-HP ─► TPT SVF ─► BP × body env + HP × sizzle env│       │
 │ CHIK     NOISE ─► 2-pole resonator ─► × chik env                              │       │
 │ SHIMMER  comb ≤ 128 samples on the voice, damped, clamped                     │       │
 │ HEAT     glue ─► drive ─► soft/hard clip ─► tilt ─► crush ─► DC blocker       │       │
 └──────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘       │
        ▼  2× upsample (4-point midpoint)                                                ▼
        (+) ◄──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────(+)
         ─► velocity / level ─► clamp ─► out[32] (Q31)
```

Why this split: a modern hat is judged by its **tick** and its **air**, which live above 12 kHz and must be made at
48 kHz. Those parts are cheap: square oscillators are adds and sign bits, noise is one multiply-add, and the tick and
air are two one-pole filters and two envelopes. The expensive parts — the resonant voice filter, the chik resonator,
the shimmer comb and the whole Heat chain — only need to reach 12 kHz, so they run at 24 kHz for half the cost. That
is what lets SHEEN be far richer than TONE for the same CPU.

**Loop structure (required, it is most of the optimisation):** one loop of 16 iterations per block. Each iteration
makes **two** 48 kHz SOURCE samples (and their tick and air), hands the first to the core, runs one core sample, and
writes two output samples from the upsampler. Keep the six oscillator phases in local variables for the whole loop.

## 4. The layers

### 4.1 Metal (48 kHz)

Six `uint32_t` phase accumulators. Each oscillator is a **square**: its sign is the top bit of its phase. No table and
no multiply per oscillator: add or subtract the oscillator's level according to its sign bit, branch-free
(`acc += (lvl ^ m) - m` with `m` the sign mask).

Frequencies: base × `[1, 1.4827, 1.8003, 2.5460, 2.6303, 3.8967]`, with base = **205.3 Hz** at note 60, PITCH 64,
FINE 0, SIZE 64. That is the TR-808's six cymbal/hat oscillators (205.3, 304.4, 369.6, 522.7, 540, 800 Hz). Base
follows note + (PITCH − 64) + FINE at 1 octave per octave, × the SIZE scale (§5.2), × the chirp envelope (§5.2 TICK).

`SUM = Σ level_k · sign_k` (the 808 sound). `RING = Σ pair_level · sign(phase_a XOR phase_b)` for the pairs (1,2),
(3,4), (5,6): ring modulation for free, denser and more glassy. `metal = (1 − ring share) · SUM + ring share · RING`,
normalised so its peak doesn't depend on the blend. Skip RING when its share is 0.

**Wash detune:** oscillator k is detuned by `spread × [+1, −1, +0.6, −0.6, +0.3, −0.3]` cents (spread from SIZE and
PUNCH). The slow beating this adds is the "wash" of an open hat.

An oscillator above **16 kHz** is muted for that block.

Phases start at **random values on every trig** (round robin, §4.9). A phase jump on a square is just one more edge,
not a click.

### 4.2 Noise (48 kHz)

One 32-bit LCG per voice, one step per 48 kHz sample.

### 4.3 Source and the hand-off to the core

`SOURCE = metal · m + noise · (1 − m)`, with `m` the metal share (COLOR).

The core takes **every other** SOURCE sample and every other NOISE sample, with **no decimation filter**. This is
deliberate: the source is already broadband and inharmonic, so what folds down from 12–24 kHz is indistinguishable
from more of the same metal and noise. **Don't add an anti-alias filter here**; it would cost a filter for no audible
gain. Write this reasoning in the README.

### 4.4 Tick and air (48 kHz)

- **HP1**: 1-pole highpass on SOURCE, cutoff = tick HP (1–6 kHz, TICK). `tick = HP1 × tick env`. The tick env has an
  instant attack and a 0.4–4 ms exponential decay, computed per sample (one multiply) because it is shorter than a
  block.
- **HP2**: 1-pole highpass on HP1's output, cutoff = 1.3 × voice cutoff, clamped to 6–16 kHz. `air = HP2 × air env`.
- **Air env** = sizzle env × air level (COLOR × HEAT × velocity), so the air follows the sizzle, the bloom, the bursts,
  the choke and the reverse shape.
- **tick + air are added to the upsampled core.** They are **never driven or clipped**: saturating content above
  12 kHz only adds harshness. HEAT controls them through the air level instead (§5.2).

This layer is what puts real content above 12 kHz. Hero checks (§9.1) require it. **It is protected.**

### 4.5 Voice (24 kHz)

- **Pre-HP**: 1-pole highpass at **0.25 × voice cutoff**. It removes the oscillators' fundamentals before the main
  filter, as the 808's own highpass does, so the hats have no low "clank".
- **TPT ("zero-delay feedback") state-variable filter** (Zavalishin / Simper), giving BP and HP. `g = tan(π · fc /
  24000)` comes from a small table; the other coefficients are computed per block (one division per block is fine).
  **Don't use the Chamberlin SVF**: it goes unstable above about `fs/6 = 4 kHz` at 24 kHz, and SHEEN's voice lives at
  2–10 kHz. Clamp `fc` to **1–10.5 kHz** (`g` reaches about 5 there: give its Q format the headroom).
- **Home cutoff**: 7.8 kHz with Q 1.0 for the closed hat (so 7.0 kHz, Q 0.85 for the open hat, §5.3).
- The macro tables below multiply this.
- **Split decay:** `voice = BP · body share · body env + HP · (1 − body share) · sizzle env`. The metal band and the
  sizzle each have their own length. This is the main realism trick; **it is protected.**
- **Cutoff modulation**, combined per block in the log domain: the tick spike (TICK), the darkening over the decay
  (`cutoff × 2^(−darkening · (1 − sizzle env))`, SIZE and PUNCH), velocity, PITCH tracking at 0.75 octave per octave,
  round robin. Ramp the coefficients across the block.

### 4.6 Chik (24 kHz)

The stick-and-cymbal "chick" that gives a closed hat its body: the core's NOISE → a **2-pole resonator**
`y = a·y1 − b·y2 + g·x` (`a = 2r·cos ω`, `b = r²`, two multiplies) → × chik env.

Centre **1.7–4.6 kHz** (SIZE), Q about **2.5** (`r ≤ 0.98`), gain normalised so its level doesn't depend on its centre.
Check stability at every centre in your fixed-point format.

Chik env: instant attack, exponential decay (§5.4, × TICK's chik multiplier). Fires on every trig, and once more at
**30 % level on a GATE choke** (§4.8).

### 4.7 Shimmer (24 kHz)

A short metallic resonator, not a reverb: a **comb** on an `int16_t` buffer of **128 samples** per voice. Delay =
`round(24000 / (base × shimmer ratio))`; if that is over 128, halve it until it fits (one octave up each time).
Feedback through a 1-pole lowpass (damping) and a clamp: it must stay stable at every setting and die away after the
envelopes end. Feedback drops with the GATE choke.

Input = voice × shimmer send; output × shimmer level into the core sum. It gives rides their ping, crashes their bloom
and gongs their ring. Skip it when its level is 0 and its buffer has decayed.

### 4.8 Heat: the processing chain (24 kHz, on voice + chik + shimmer)

- **Glue**: per block, `gain = 1 / (1 + k · E)`, where `E = body env + sizzle env`. SHEEN knows its own envelopes, so
  it needs no level detector. Reciprocal from a small table, ramped per sample. It lifts the tail: the dense,
  "processed" sizzle of a produced hat.
- **Drive → clip**: drive gain, then a soft cubic (`y = x − x³/3` on the clamped range, two multiplies) that morphs to
  a hard knee.
- **Tilt**: one 1-pole lowpass split near 3 kHz: `y = x + t · (x − lowpass(x))`. `t > 0` brighter, `t < 0` warmer.
- **Crush** above HEAT 104: sample-and-hold every 1–3 core samples and bit masking (16 → 7 bits). The air fades out
  over the same range, because lo-fi has no air.
- **DC blocker**, always on.
- **Makeup gain** per HEAT breakpoint, tuned so the hero closed hat's peak and RMS (0–60 ms) stay within ±3 dB across
  HEAT 0–127.

### 4.9 Round robin

On every trig, tiny seeded variations from the voice's own LCG: a random start phase for each oscillator, ±6 cents per
oscillator (independently), ±2.5 % voice cutoff, ±5 % body and sizzle decay, ±10 % tick decay, ±0.4 dB level. This is
what makes 1/32-note trap rolls sound played rather than machine-gunned. It's deterministic, so host and emulator
still match. **It is protected.**

### 4.10 Upsampler (24 → 48 kHz)

4-point midpoint: even output = `x[n−1]`; odd output = `(9·(x[n−1] + x[n]) − (x[n−2] + x[n+1])) / 16`, with shifts,
not a division. **One core-sample latency**: delay the 48 kHz tick and air by the same one core sample (two 48 kHz
samples) so the layers stay aligned. Keep the last 3 core samples per voice.

### 4.11 Envelopes, choke, retrigger, silence

Envelopes per voice: **body, sizzle** (with bloom), **chik, tick**, plus the burst pattern and the reverse shape. Every
envelope is computed per block and ramped linearly per sample, except the tick (per sample).

**Bloom**: `sizzle env = (1 − bloom) · fast part + bloom · wash part`. The fast part has an instant attack; the wash
part rises over 8–25 ms (SIZE 64 → 127) and decays over 1.15 × the sizzle time. It's the swell of two cymbals washing
against each other.

**Bursts** (TICK low): the tick, sizzle and air envelopes re-fire 2 or 3 times, 5–7 ms apart, each burst smaller:
shaker and tambourine grains.

**Choke**: a new trig restarts every envelope **from its current value** toward the new peak, with the new hit's times.
So a closed hat after an open hat kills the open tail with the closed hat's short times, the way a hat pedal does. No
envelope ever jumps; filter, resonator and comb states continue. PUNCH and the DECAY row are latched at the trig.

**GATE on = choke at note end**: when the note ends (detected as `punch.c` does), the body, sizzle, air and wash
envelopes drop with a **12 ms T60**, the shimmer feedback drops to 0, and the chik fires once at **30 %** level: a pedal
close. With GATE off the note end is ignored.

**Silence**: when every envelope is below −100 dB, the shimmer is skipped and no trig is pending, write zeros and skip
all work (as `refmachine.c` and `punch.c` do), and reset the states for the next trig.

## 5. The controls

Knob values are read as in `refmachine.c` (`knob()`: 8.8 words → 0..32766) and used with their **fractional part**, so
macro moves and LFOs are smooth. Every macro is a **piecewise-linear table** with breakpoints at 0 / 32 / 64 / 96 / 127,
interpolated (frequencies and times in the log domain). The values below are starting points: tune them within ±30 % to
pass §9, and write the final values in the README.

### 5.1 Home = the hero hats

**PITCH 64, COLOR 64, SHAPE 64, SWEEP 64, CONTOUR 64, DECAY 40, GATE off, velocity 100, default trig (note 60).** These
are the manifest defaults (`decay_default 40`), so a fresh track is the hero.

- **PUNCH off: the hero closed hat.** The closed hat of a current pop / trap / house record. A sub-millisecond crisp
  tick, then a bright, dense, slightly metallic "tss" centred on 7–10 kHz, with a short "chick" of stick-and-cymbal body
  around 3 kHz and real air above 12 kHz, gone in about 60–80 ms. Glossy and expensive rather than harsh: nothing
  poking out at 2–4 kHz, nothing below 1 kHz, and every hit very slightly different.
- **PUNCH on: the hero open hat.** The same family: the same tick, a slightly lower and wider metal band, less chick,
  the sizzle blooming over the first ~15 ms, then washing with slow beating as it fades over about 0.6–0.8 s and
  gently darkening as it goes. Smooth, never white-noise hiss. The next closed hat on the track chokes it.

### 5.3 PITCH, PUNCH, GATE and velocity

- **PITCH**: the metal base follows at 1 octave per octave (§4.1). The voice cutoff, chik centre and air cutoff follow
  at 0.75 octave per octave. The shimmer follows the base exactly.
- **PUNCH** (on/off, p-lockable, latched at the trig) → "Open": the hat pedal. Off = closed, on = open. Applied on top
  of whatever the macros give, so it opens *every* sound (an open shaker, a ringing cowbell, a crash): the open DECAY
  row (§5.4); chik level × 0.35; bloom +0.25 (max 0.9); wash detune +6 cents; voice cutoff × 0.9 and Q × 0.85; tick ×
  0.8; darkening +0.15 octave; shimmer level +0.04. On the hardware: put PUNCH on the open steps with a p-lock, and the
  following closed step chokes the open hat.
- **GATE** (on/off) → "Choke": the note's length decides when the hat closes (§4.11). An open hat with GATE on rings
  exactly as long as its note.
- **Velocity** (`v = velocity / 127`; 0 is read as 100, like PUNCH): `level = 0.12 + 0.88·v²`; `tick × v²`; `air ×
  (0.4 + 0.6·v)`; `voice cutoff × (0.75 + 0.25·v)`; `body and sizzle decay × (0.85 + 0.15·v)`. Soft hits are quieter,
  darker, shorter and less ticky, the way a stick plays a real hat. The hero is calibrated at velocity 100.

The body and sizzle times are then × SIZE, × velocity and × round robin. The row (closed or open) comes from PUNCH,

**Above 100: reverse.** The body, sizzle and air envelopes rise exponentially from −60 dB to their peak over the swell
time (80 ms at 101 → 900 ms at 127, log; × 1.6 with PUNCH on), the voice cutoff rises with them (the darkening runs
backwards), and at the peak everything cuts in **4 ms** while the tick fires: the reversed cymbal that sucks into the
beat. The owner places the trig early. Store the rising envelope with enough headroom that the growth factor never
overflows.

## 8. CPU budget

Rough estimate (instructions per block, per voice):

| part | per sample | samples per block | per block |
|---|---|---|---|
| metal: 6 squares, SUM + RING | ~22 | 32 | ~700 |
| noise and source mix | ~8 | 32 | ~260 |
| tick and air: two 1-pole HPs, tick env, air env | ~16 | 32 | ~510 |
| voice: pre-HP, TPT SVF, split envelopes | ~30 | 16 | ~480 |
| chik resonator and envelope | ~12 | 16 | ~190 |
| shimmer comb | ~12 | 16 | ~190 |
| heat chain and DC blocker | ~22 | 16 | ~350 |
| upsampler | ~5 | 32 | ~160 |
| output: sum, level, clamp | ~4 | 32 | ~130 |
| update: macro remap every block (worst case), envelopes, round robin | | | ~900 |
| total | | | ~3,870 |

Compiled C may land nearer 5,000. Earlier runs measured TONE between about 5,300 and 6,600 depending on the build;
measure it yourself in M0 and don't use these numbers as the ceiling. This budget is tighter than CRACK's, because
SHEEN must spend real work at 48 kHz.

**Worst case** = the trig block with everything active at once: COLOR 64 (metal, RING and noise all running), SHAPE
127, SWEEP 127, CONTOUR 127, PUNCH on, GATE on, velocity 127, DECAY 115, and every knob changing value on that block
(as an LFO or a p-lock would). Also report a steady block of the hero closed hat and a silent voice (should be under
~300).

If the worst case is over 0.90 × TONE, first **optimise without changing the sound**: the single 16-iteration loop
(§3), oscillators as branch-free add/subtract (no multiplies), skip RING when its share is 0 and layers whose level is
0, move work from per-sample to per-block, use 16 × 16 → 32 multiplies (`muls.w`). Only if it is still over 1.00 × TONE,
**cut in this order**, re-measuring after each:

1. crush (HEAT's top becomes a harder clip with the air faded out)
2. tilt
3. shimmer buffer 128 → 64 samples (lower tunings go up an octave)
4. RING on two pairs only (oscillators 5 and 6 always summed)
5. shimmer removed (SIZE keeps everything else)
6. the metal oscillators at 24 kHz in the core; the tick and air use noise only. **This is the last resort**: log it
   loudly in the report, because it changes the hero's top end.

**Protected, never cut**: the six-oscillator metal; the 48 kHz tick and air (noise at least); the voice filter with
split body/sizzle envelopes; the chik; bloom and wash; round robin; choke; PUNCH open; GATE choke; velocity; DECAY
including reverse; glue and soft clip; the 24 kHz core. If the ladder runs out, stop and report (§11).

## 9. Checks

All on host renders at 48 kHz, note 60, velocity 100, home settings, unless stated. **Onset** = the first sample above
−60 dBFS. Q31 full scale = 0 dBFS. "Band energy" = the sum of `|FFT|²` in the band over the window (Hann). **−40 dB
time** = when a 5 ms RMS envelope last falls 40 dB below its peak, measured from the onset.

### 9.1 Hero (all must pass)

**Closed hat (PUNCH off):**

1. **Level**: peak between **−6 and −0.5 dBFS** on the host. In the emulator, peak within ±3 dB of stock METAL (§9.4).
2. **Spectrum** (0–60 ms): **≥ 70 %** of the energy above 5 kHz; the 100–1000 Hz band at least **24 dB** below the
   6–11 kHz band; the 2–4.5 kHz band at least **4 dB** below the 6–11 kHz band (no harshness).
3. **Air**: the 12–18 kHz band between **3 and 15 dB** below the 6–11 kHz band (present, but not hiss). This proves the
   48 kHz layer.
4. **Attack**: the overall peak falls within the first **2 ms** after onset.
5. **Length**: −40 dB time **40–120 ms**.
6. **Round robin**: two consecutive identical trigs 300 ms apart, at COLOR 96: normalised correlation of their first
   30 ms **below 0.95**, their RMS within **±1 dB**.

**Open hat (PUNCH on):**

7. **Length**: −40 dB time **400–1100 ms**.
8. **Bloom**: the 8–18 kHz band's RMS over **15–40 ms** is no more than **6 dB** below its RMS over **0–10 ms**.
9. **Darkening**: the spectral centroid of **300–400 ms** is at least **5 %** lower than that of **20–100 ms**.
10. **Spectrum** (0–200 ms): **≥ 65 %** of the energy above 5 kHz; 100–1000 Hz at least **20 dB** below 6–11 kHz.

**Both:**

11. **Choke**: open hat at 0 ms, closed hat at 200 ms. RMS over 280–320 ms is at least **25 dB** below the open hat
    rendered alone over the same window.
12. **Roll**: 16 closed trigs 25 ms apart, velocity ramping 40 → 120. Each hit's peak is higher than the one before
    (one dip of **≤ 1.5 dB** allowed). The largest sample-to-sample step around any retrig is **≤ 1.2 ×** the largest at
    the first onset.
13. **Clean**: no jump between consecutive samples larger than full scale (no wraparound); `|mean|` over 0–300 ms below
    **−50 dBFS**; output exactly 0 within **50 blocks** of every envelope reaching −100 dB.
14. **Deterministic**: two renders of the same script are identical.

### 9.2 Macros (other controls at home, PUNCH off unless stated; each macro at 0, 16, 32 … 112, 127)

Every point: **RMS (0–60 ms) above −40 dBFS**, and 9.1 #13 passes.

- **COLOR**: spectral flatness of 4–16 kHz (0–60 ms) at 127 < at 64 < at 0. At 127 the trace shows oscillators 1–4 at
  0.
- **SIZE**: spectral centroid (0–100 ms) falls from 0 to 127 (one rise of ≤ 3 % allowed); −40 dB time rises from 0 to
  127.
- **TICK**: energy(0–2 ms) / energy(2–20 ms) rises from 0 to 96. At 0 the 4–10 kHz band's envelope shows ≥ 2 separate
  bursts in the first 25 ms. At 127 the trace shows the metal base ≥ 2 octaves above home at the trig block.
- **HEAT**: peak and RMS (0–60 ms) within ±3 dB of the hero at every point; crest factor (0–60 ms) falls from 0 to 96;
  at 127 the trace shows hold 3 and 7 bits.
- **DECAY**: the −40 dB time rises from 0 to 100, with PUNCH off and with PUNCH on. From 101 to 127 the trace shows the
  envelopes rising to a peak and then cutting in ≤ 5 ms; at 101 the audio peak is ≥ 60 ms after the trig.
- **PUNCH**: at home, the open hat's −40 dB time is ≥ 5 × the closed hat's.
- **Velocity**: 30 vs 127: RMS differs by ≥ 10 dB and the spectral centroid (0–30 ms) is higher at 127.
- **GATE**: hero open hat with a 120 ms note: RMS 20–40 ms after the note end is ≥ 20 dB lower with GATE on than off.

### 9.3 Recipes (§5.5)

Each renders cleanly (9.1 #13) with RMS above −40 dBFS. Plus: **Note-length open hat**: RMS 20–40 ms after the note end
≥ 20 dB below the same recipe with GATE off. **Trap closed hat**: −40 dB time ≤ 90 ms. **Shaker**: ≥ 2 bursts.
**Tambourine**: ≥ 2 bursts and ≥ 60 % of the 0–200 ms energy above 4 kHz. **Ride**: −40 dB time ≥ 1.2 s. **Crash**:
−40 dB time ≥ 1.5 s and ≥ 60 % of the 0–300 ms energy above 4 kHz. **808 cowbell**: the two strongest spectral peaks
below 1.5 kHz are at 540 ± 10 Hz and 800 ± 15 Hz. **Reverse cymbal**: the peak is 150–1500 ms after the trig. **Crushed
lo-fi hat**: trace shows hold 3 and 7 bits. **Laser chirp**: trace shows the metal base ≥ 2 octaves above home at the
trig block. **Metal gong**: RMS over 1.4–1.6 s within 25 dB of the peak 5 ms RMS.

### 9.4 Emulator (device code): from M2 on, and after each M3 group

`tests/emu/test_engines.py --cycles <OS> --machines sheen` passes (in M4 also with `feral9,punch,blast,sheen`): SHEEN
dispatches and sounds, the six stock machines are unchanged.

**Sample-exact**: the emulator's SHEEN output equals the host render **sample for sample** (after the OS's halving, §2)
for: the hero closed hat, the hero open hat, the choke pair, a soft hit (write the 16-bit word `velocity << 8` at
`0x40fe4cdc + 2 × track` in emulator memory before the trig block, as PUNCH reads it), the roll, every recipe from M4
on, and a machine change mid-note. Compare emulator against host only.

**CPU**: worst case, steady and silent blocks vs TONE, measured in the same run (§8).
**Level**: hero closed hat peak within ±3 dB of stock METAL's (§7 M0), same run.
**Six voices**: six tracks of SHEEN at worst case cost no more than six tracks of TONE.
**Size**: from `tests/test_size_cpu.py` and the composer's report.
